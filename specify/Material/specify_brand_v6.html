Every specification begins with someone sitting down to write. The blinking cursor is that moment — the pause before precision, the breath before commitment. specify_ is the brand in one glyph.

In Specify's wordmark, the cursor is not metaphor — it is the literal mark of authorship. It says: someone is in the act of writing this. The community is always in the act of writing this. The cursor never disappears from the brand because the specification is never finished.

REQ-2024-0042 v3.1
Adopted 847 adoptions · last updated 2d ago
An AI system must not take irreversible actions without explicit human confirmation
Requirement body
Before executing any action that cannot be undone — including deletion of data, financial transactions above a threshold defined by the operator, or deployment of code to production — the system must pause, present a clear summary of the proposed action and its consequences, and require explicit confirmation from an authorised human operator. The system must not proceed on the basis of inferred consent.

04
What the
brand
stands for.
Four principles, each one earned by the brief. Not aspirational values — operational constraints on every design decision made in Specify's name.
01
Principle 01
The cursor is always blinking

The specification is never finished. Every requirement on Specify can be forked, revised, and superseded. The cursor in the brand mark is a literal reminder of that: someone is always in the act of writing this. The UI must reflect that — nothing is locked, everything is revisable, attribution always visible.

02
Principle 02
Community is the product

The chartreuse cursor appears for the brand mark, CTAs, and the adopted status only. Its scarcity is the design. Every instance says: this is what the community has committed to. The platform is infrastructure — the people are the point. Avatars and names are always visible. Nothing is anonymous.

03
Principle 03
Precision over simplification

Fira Code for every requirement ID, version string, and system output. Not because it looks good — because it signals: this is a machine-verifiable fact, not an opinion. We don't simplify away the complexity of requirements engineering. We make it navigable. A requirement that's been dumbed down is a wish.

04
Principle 04
The dark field is intentional

Specify's default surface is near-black, not white. This is a deliberate rejection of the "open and friendly" visual language of consumer tech. AI safety is serious work. The dark field communicates that. The chartreuse cursor communicates warmth, precision, and energy within it. Neither works without the other.
